Euler's identity

A mathematical relationship, a way to study its form, and a clear distinction between proof and personal interpretation.

The mathematical statement

Euler's formula states that eiθ = cos(θ) + i sin(θ). Substituting θ = π gives cos(π) = −1 and sin(π) = 0. Therefore eiπ = −1, and eiπ + 1 = 0.

This equality is a mathematical fact. It does not, on its own, establish claims about physics, consciousness, theology, or the origins of the universe.

What is established

The formula follows from the properties of complex exponentiation and trigonometry. Its proof is mathematical, not experimental evidence for an unrelated hypothesis.

What remains interpretive

Unity, symmetry and return may be useful philosophical or artistic metaphors. They are reflections, not new scientific results or proof of a spiritual claim.

A repeatable way to read any strong claim

  1. State the claim precisely and identify whether it is mathematics, science, history, philosophy, or personal interpretation.
  2. Show the steps or identify the evidence that genuinely supports it.
  3. Describe the limit: what does that evidence not establish?
  4. Keep new hypotheses open to independent review instead of relabeling reflection as proof.
